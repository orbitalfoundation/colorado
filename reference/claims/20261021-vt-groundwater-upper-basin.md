# 2026-10-21 — Upper Basin groundwater loss (Oyedele & Werth, WRR)

Sent by Anselm 2026-10-21 via a r/science thread (post 1xbj39b, which
could not be read from this box: Reddit returns 403). Facts below are
from the Virginia Tech press release only; the paper itself is behind
Wiley's bot wall and was not read.

**Source:** Kelly Izlar, "Colorado River drought drives unprecedented
pattern of groundwater loss", Virginia Tech News, 2026-10-13.
https://news.vt.edu/articles/2026/10/science-crb-groundwater-loss.html

**Study:** Esther Oyedele (lead, geosciences graduate student) and
Susanna Werth (assistant professor of hydrology and remote sensing),
Virginia Tech, with a team of geoscientists. *Water Resources Research*,
DOI 10.1029/2025WR041695. Method: NASA GRACE gravity data combined with
estimates of surface water, soil moisture and snow-water storage,
2004 to 2023, to isolate groundwater change in each basin.

**Findings as stated in the release:**
- Groundwater depletion accelerated after 2019.
- 2020 to 2022 drought: Upper Basin lost about 4.6 trillion gallons of
  groundwater, nearly double the Lower Basin's estimated 2.5 trillion.
- Historically the Lower Basin lost more; the release calls this a
  turning point. Groundwater "did not track surface-water shortages in
  the same way" in the two basins (Oyedele).
- Werth: continued Upper Basin depletion "could further reduce
  groundwater contributions to rivers and intensify the feedback between
  groundwater loss and surface-water shortages"; surface water alone is
  no longer enough to manage.

**Conversion (mine, not in the release):** 1 acre-foot = 325,851 gal.
4.6 trillion gal ≈ 14.1 MAF; 2.5 trillion gal ≈ 7.7 MAF; both over the
three drought years. Error bars are not given in the release.

**Why it matters here:** this is the first citable source for the
Upper/Lower flip, and the basin-scale number behind the
groundwater-substitution lever (open thread 3 in
devlog/20260902-state-of-the-work.md; brief.md "hidden debt, delayed
consequence"). It also complicates the Upper Basin's claim that it
already takes its cuts hydrologically: part of the shortfall has been
covered from storage nobody meters.

**Companion source, earlier:** ASU-led GRACE study, reported 2025-05-30
(KJZZ) and 2025-06-02 (KUNC/Grist): about 27.8 MAF of basin groundwater
lost since 2003, roughly a full Lake Mead, groundwater losses 2.4x
surface-reservoir losses, most of it in Arizona, California and Nevada.
Not yet read or verified here; flagged for the verification ledger.

**Caveats:** GRACE footprint is coarse (hundreds of km); groundwater is
a residual after subtracting modeled stores, so the split carries real
uncertainty. Verify the paper's own numbers before anything ships.
