**The 1968 warnings, as the article gives them:**
- Floyd Dominy (Commissioner of Reclamation), at the CAP signing:
  available supply would be more than halved between 1990 and 2030. He
  blamed upstream growth in CO, NM, UT, WY; the article says drought and
  climate change became the driver instead.
- Mo Udall (AZ congressman), 1968 testimony: "Let's assume there is no
  augmentation in the river — not a drop", calling the assumption "very
  violent", and that he was "as sure as anything in this life"
  augmentation would come.
- Stewart Udall (Interior Secretary 1961–69) also tied the CAP's future
  to augmentation: a Columbia River link, Pacific desalination, a
  cross-basin pipeline. Pacific Northwest senators wrote a ten-year bar
  on federal study of importing water from another river system into the
  authorizing act.
- Signing: 1968-09-20, Lyndon Johnson with Sen. Carl Hayden (photo).

**Why ignored (named voices):** Tom McCann, former CAP deputy GM: "no
willingness to spend money to address a future problem that wasn't here
yet." Brad Udall (CSU; Mo's son, Stewart's nephew): the Udalls "sort of
blew it", with postwar optimism and fatigue after the Arizona v.
California fight as mitigation. Chuck Podolak (ex-director, Water
Infrastructure Finance Authority of Arizona): promising augmentation let
politicians avoid saying Phoenix would grow at agriculture's expense;
the 1960s documents are the work of "fallible people" and not sacred,
but ocean desal still deserves a look. John Berggren (Western Resource
Advocates; PhD on the CAP's conception): the river has a "structural
deficit", everyone must use less.

**Present tense in the article:** farms and ranches use roughly
three-quarters of the river. The CAP is a 336-mile system. The new
federal plan "could cut the CAP's allocation by 47%" (baseline not
stated). Nevada, Arizona and the CAP have taken legal action. Cities
warn of billions for adaptation; Phoenix is spending on advanced water
purification; the CAP is exploring a transfer deal with the San Diego
County Water Authority; WIFA was promised $1 billion in 2022 for new
supply, much of it reportedly withheld. Tribes get a photo caption
(Gila River Indian Community alfalfa) and nothing else.

**Lawsuit timeline (from a one-pass news search, not the docket):**
Nevada (SNWA, Colorado River Commission of Nevada, the state) sued
Reclamation over the post-2026 framework around 2026-08-25; Arizona
(ADWR) moved to intervene 2026-10-16; the CAP board said 2026-10-27 it
is seeking to join. The framework took effect 2026-10-01; Arizona's
delivery cuts begin 2027-01-01.

**Cut-size discrepancy, unresolved:** press gives 47% (KJZZ), 50%
(another outlet), "77% over two years without the Lower Basin
agreement", and "nearly a third" of Arizona's share. Our ledger has
AZ 760 KAF = 27% (docs/verification.md). These are different baselines
(CAP vs Arizona, with vs without the states' deal). This is the same
